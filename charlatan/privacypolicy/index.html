Privacy Policy

Effective 2026-11-26 · Charlatan for iPhone and iPad

The short version

Charlatan has no account, no sign-up and no server of its own. Your videos, your audio and your subtitles stay on your iPhone or iPad: they are never uploaded, and none of them is ever included in the statistics below.

The app is free and shows ads through Google AdMob, which picks each ad from Google or from three other ad networks it works with, Unity Ads, Liftoff Monetize and Meta Audience Network. You can remove them with a paid plan. To serve and measure the ads, their advertising software inside the app receives some technical information from your device (section 5).

To fix crashes and see how the app is used, it sends us usage statistics and crash reports through Google Firebase, linked to a random identifier and never to you (section 3). Nothing is collected until you accept these terms and this policy the first time you open the app.

1. Who this policy covers

This policy applies to the Charlatan app for iPhone and iPad (“the app”) and to this page. The app is published by Nicolas Gula, an independent developer based in Uruguay, under the dezeo name (“we”, “us”). For the purposes of data protection law we are the controller of any personal data processed through the app. It does not cover the App Store, whose terms and privacy policy apply to your download and purchases.

2. What never leaves your device

Charlatan transcribes your video's audio using Apple's speech recognition and requires it to run entirely on the device. The app only offers the languages the device can transcribe on its own; there is no server option and no fallback to one, so the audio never leaves your phone.

The camera and microphone are used only when you record a video inside the app. Photo library access is used only to pick the videos you choose and to save your exports. Your projects (the video references, transcripts and styles) are stored in the app's private storage and are deleted when you delete them or uninstall the app. We have no copy and no way to access them.

Charlatan does not collect, detect, analyse, derive or store face data of any kind. The app performs no face detection, face recognition, face tracking or facial-geometry mapping, and it does not use ARKit, the Vision framework, Face ID or any other biometric API. A video you record or import is treated as a video file: its audio is transcribed on the device, and the frames are only decoded to show a preview and to render your subtitles into the exported file.

Subtitle translation uses Apple's Translation framework, which may download language models and processes your subtitle text according to Apple's privacy policy. Charlatan does not send your text to any server of its own: it has none. Picking the key word of each line and the emoji suggestions also run on the device.

3. Data we collect

We do not collect your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, videos, audio, subtitles, precise location or anything else that tells us who you are. What does reach us comes through Google Firebase, a set of tools from Google LLC that processes the data on our behalf, and is limited to the following.

Usage statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase). Only after you accept the Terms of Use and this policy, the app records which parts of it are used: the screens you open; that you chose a video (from the library, the camera or the share sheet); that a transcription started, finished or failed, with its language, the length of the video in seconds and the number of subtitles; which editor tab you open; that an export started, finished or failed, with its length in seconds; whether you open the plans, which plan you choose and that the ads were removed; how the ads behave (loaded, shown, closed, failed) and what Google estimates each impression to be worth; your choice about personalized ads; and your app language and whether the ads are removed. It never includes the content of a video, of the audio or of a subtitle.

With these events Google collects a random app-instance identifier created when the app is installed; the device model, operating system, language and app version; the number and length of sessions; and an approximate location (country, region or city) derived from your IP address, which Google Analytics does not store. We see this mostly as totals, for example how many people finish an export or at which step they stop opening the app, and we use it to decide what to improve and to check that a change made the app better. On iOS, Analytics can read the advertising identifier (IDFA) only if you allowed tracking in Apple's prompt. If you turn off Personalized ads in Settings, the statistics are not used to personalize ads.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, usage statistics are stored only if you agree to it in the consent message the app shows before any ads, and they are used for advertising only if you agree to that too. If you decline, Google Analytics receives only signals without identifiers, from which it can estimate totals. You can change your answer at any time in Settings → Ad privacy choices.

Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics). If the app crashes or runs into an error, it sends a report with the technical trace of the error, the device model, operating system, free memory and storage, orientation, app version and a random installation identifier. Crash reports contain no video, audio or subtitle and nothing you wrote. We use them only to find and fix bugs.

Values we can adjust (Firebase Remote Config). When it starts, the app asks Firebase for a few numbers we can change without an update, such as how often a full-screen ad appears. Firebase receives a random installation identifier, the app version, language and country.

If you write to us for support, we receive what you choose to send: your email address and the content of your message. We use it only to answer you and keep it only as long as needed to resolve your request. Please do not send us personal information we do not need.

4. What the app stores on your device

Your projects, your saved styles, your choices (language, subtitle pace, personalized ads) and a few bookkeeping values such as how many exports you have made, which decides when the app offers to remove the ads. None of this is sent to us. Deleting the app removes all of it, immediately and permanently, and a new install starts with a new random identifier.

5. Advertising (Google AdMob, Unity Ads, Liftoff Monetize, Meta Audience Network)

The app is free and is supported by ads delivered by Google AdMob, a service of Google LLC. For each ad, AdMob holds an auction between Google's own ads and three other ad networks that bid for it, Unity Ads (Unity Technologies), Liftoff Monetize (Liftoff Mobile, formerly Vungle) and Meta Audience Network (Meta Platforms), and shows the one that pays the most. It shows two kinds: a banner at the bottom of the home screen and full-screen ads while a video loads, when a saved video is reopened and while the subtitles are burned into it (one when you load a video, one when you reopen a saved one, two when you export). Never while you are editing, and never over your video. You can remove all of them with the monthly plan (while it lasts) or the lifetime plan, Charlatan PRO.

What the ad networks' software collects. To deliver ads, limit how often you see them, measure their performance and detect fraud, the advertising software of each network inside the app (the Google Mobile Ads SDK, the Unity Ads SDK, the Liftoff Monetize SDK and the Meta Audience Network SDK) collects technical information and sends it directly to that network. It can include your IP address, from which an approximate location can be inferred (we never receive it); device information such as model, operating system version, language, screen size, time zone and network type; information about the ads shown and how you interact with them; diagnostic information about the ad software; and your device's advertising identifier, where you have allowed it. Google may use this to show personalized ads, based on interests inferred across apps, or non-personalized ads, based only on context. Google processes it under its own policies, not ours; see how Google uses data when you use its partners’ sites or apps and Google's Privacy Policy. Unity Ads, Liftoff Monetize and Meta Audience Network use the information the same way: to bid for an ad, show it, measure it and prevent fraud, and to personalize it where you allow that. Each processes it under its own policy, not ours: see Unity's privacy policy for game players and app users, Liftoff's privacy policy and Meta's privacy policy.

Your choices about ads. The first time ads are about to be shown, the app asks for permission to track you with Apple's standard prompt. If you decline, no ad network receives your device's advertising identifier from the app, and the ads are non-personalized. You can change this at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, personalized ads are only shown with your consent: the app asks for your choice before loading any ads and you can change it in Settings → Ad privacy choices; that message lists every ad partner, Unity Ads, Liftoff Monetize and Meta Audience Network among them, and passes your answer to each. Everywhere, the Personalized ads switch in the app's Settings turns personalization off: Google then serves non-personalized ads, and Unity Ads, Liftoff Monetize and Meta Audience Network are told that you opted out of the sale or sharing of your data for advertising. Buying a plan stops the ads. Choosing non-personalized ads does not remove ads: the app still needs them to stay free.

6. Purchases

Removing the ads is sold through Apple's App Store as a monthly auto-renewing subscription or as a one-time lifetime purchase (Charlatan PRO). Payment is handled entirely by Apple; we never see your payment details. The app checks what you are entitled to with Apple's StoreKit, on the device, and sends us only that a purchase happened, with its product identifier, as a usage statistic (section 3). “Restore purchases” asks Apple to send your past purchases again.

7. Third parties that receive data

Only the following companies receive data from the app, and only in the ways described above. We do not sell personal data, and we do not share it with anyone else for their own marketing.

  • Google LLC · Firebase (Analytics, Crashlytics, Remote Config): usage statistics, crash reports and adjustable values; acts as our processor. Privacy and Security in Firebase.
  • Google LLC · Google Mobile Ads (AdMob) and Google’s consent message: showing and measuring ads, fraud prevention and asking for your choice. How Google uses data from partner apps.
  • Unity Technologies · Unity Ads: bidding for and showing ads through AdMob, measuring them and fraud prevention. Unity Privacy Policy for game players and app users.
  • Liftoff Mobile, Inc. · Liftoff Monetize (formerly Vungle): bidding for and showing ads through AdMob, measuring them and fraud prevention. Liftoff Privacy Policy.
  • Meta Platforms, Inc. · Meta Audience Network: bidding for and showing ads through AdMob, measuring them and fraud prevention. Meta Privacy Policy.
  • Apple Inc. · App Store, StoreKit, speech recognition and Translation: download, purchases, restore, and the on-device features described in section 2. Apple Privacy Policy.

If we ever add a service to this list, we will update this page before the change ships, and the App Store privacy details for the app will be updated to match. We may also disclose information if required by law, a court order or a public authority, or when reasonably necessary to protect our rights, your safety or the safety of others. If the app were ever transferred to another company, this policy would continue to apply to any data transferred with it.

8. Children

Charlatan is a general-audience app. It is not directed at children under 13, or under 16 in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom, or under the age at which your country lets you consent to data processing. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children, and ads in the app are not targeted at children. If you are a parent or guardian and believe a child has used the app, deleting it from the device erases everything it stored. If you believe any information about a child has reached Google through the app, write to contact@dezeo.lat and we will help you, including by pointing you to Google's own tools.

9. Legal bases (EEA, UK and Switzerland)

Where the GDPR or the UK GDPR applies, we rely on the following legal bases.

  • Running the app, applying your purchases, restoring them: performance of a contract (Article 6(1)(b)).
  • Showing non-personalized ads to keep the app free; preventing ad fraud: our legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)), balanced against your rights.
  • Showing personalized ads; any use of your advertising identifier for that purpose: your consent (Article 6(1)(a)), which you can withdraw at any time as described in section 5.
  • Usage statistics (section 3): your consent (Article 6(1)(a)), given in the app's consent message and withdrawable at any time in Settings.
  • Crash reports and adjustable values (section 3): our legitimate interests in keeping the app working and fixing its errors (Article 6(1)(f)); you can object as described in section 11.
  • Answering a support request: our legitimate interest in helping you, and your request itself.
  • Keeping records or disclosing data when the law requires it: legal obligation (Article 6(1)(c)).

10. Where data is processed

We keep no servers of our own. The statistics and crash reports in section 3 are stored by Google on its Firebase infrastructure, the store data described above is processed by Google and Apple on their own infrastructure, and the advertising data in section 5 by Google, Unity, Liftoff and Meta on theirs. These include servers in the United States and other countries whose laws may differ from yours. For data from the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google, Apple, Unity, Liftoff and Meta rely on recognized transfer safeguards, such as the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework and the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses, as described in their own privacy policies.

11. Your rights and how to use them

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access the personal data held about you, to have it corrected or deleted, to receive a copy of it, to restrict or object to its processing, to withdraw a consent you gave, and not to be discriminated against for exercising these rights.

In practice: we hold nothing that tells us who you are. The Firebase data in section 3 is tied to a random identifier that we cannot connect to you, so we cannot pick out which records are yours; it is deleted automatically after the periods in section 12, and deleting the app stops all collection. If you object to the statistics, write to us and we will help. For the advertising data processed by Google, Unity, Liftoff and Meta, the controls in section 5 let you reset or remove your advertising identifier and switch to non-personalized ads, and Google's own tools at myaccount.google.com let you review and delete activity linked to a Google account.

You can write to us at contact@dezeo.lat with “Privacy request” in the subject. We answer within 30 days (45 days for requests under California law), and we may ask you to confirm details so that we do not act on someone else's request. You can appeal our answer by replying to it. We do not make decisions about you by automated means that have legal or similarly significant effects.

EEA, United Kingdom and Switzerland: you may lodge a complaint with your data protection authority: the authority of your EU country, the UK Information Commissioner’s Office or the Swiss FDPIC.

California and other U.S. states: we do not sell personal information. Under the California Consumer Privacy Act, the disclosure of an advertising identifier to Google, Unity, Liftoff or Meta for personalized advertising may count as “sharing” for cross-context behavioral advertising. You can opt out at any time by declining tracking on iOS or turning off Personalized ads in Settings, after which the app shows only non-personalized ads and tells Unity Ads, Liftoff Monetize and Meta Audience Network that you opted out. We do not knowingly share the personal information of anyone under 16.

Brazil: under the LGPD you have the rights listed above, and you may complain to the Autoridade Nacional de Proteção de Dados (ANPD). Uruguay: under Law 18.331 you have the rights of access, rectification, update, inclusion and deletion, and you may complain to the Unidad Reguladora y de Control de Datos Personales (URCDP).

12. Data retention

The data described in section 4 stays on your device until you delete the app. Usage statistics are kept at event level for 2 months and then only as totals; crash reports are kept for 90 days. Support emails are deleted once your request is resolved, unless a law requires us to keep them longer. Advertising and store data is kept by Google, Unity, Liftoff, Meta and Apple according to their own retention policies.

13. Security

The app communicates with Google and Apple only over encrypted connections, and it stores nothing of yours anywhere but your device. We keep no database of our own; the Firebase data is stored by Google and can be reached only through our Google account. No system can be guaranteed to be completely secure; if we ever learned of a security incident affecting the app's users, we would publish a notice on this page and in the app's release notes, and notify the authorities where the law requires it.

14. This website

This website (dezeo.lat) uses Google Analytics to count visits and see which pages help. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland it runs only after you accept it, and it never runs if your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal. The details, and the place to change your choice, are on the website privacy page. None of this happens inside the app.

15. Changes to this policy

If the app's data practices change, for example because a new service is added, this page will be updated before the change ships, and the effective date above will change with it. Material changes will also be mentioned in the app's release notes. Continuing to use the app after a change means you have read the updated policy; if you disagree with it, you can stop using the app and delete it, which also deletes everything it stored.
